# Meal Plan — implementation plan

**Status:** planned, not started. Owner-requested 2026-08-11.
**Domain:** `nutrition` (secondary: `platform` for the AI route, `app-shell` for the check-in card).
**Depends on:** the calibrated calorie balance shipped 2026-08-11 (`lib/health/energy-balance-service.ts`,
`packages/shared/src/nutrition/calorie-balance.ts`, `adaptive-tdee.ts`). The plan's calorie target
comes from that service — it must not re-derive one.

---

## What the owner asked for

> Set up a "Meal Plan". It checks location and lists local supermarkets (Coles / Woolworths / Aldi).
> It gives a list of proteins / carbs / fats / vegetables to select which ones you don't want. Then
> it checks your daily calorie goal (adjusting for exercise) and asks how many meals to split into
> — ideally a scientifically backed default. It should ask when you typically train so it can prime
> the macro type around it. Save it to a table, allow editing, mark a plan active or not, and
> check in every ~4 weeks on the active plan to see if adjustments are needed.
>
> For now it can live as a section showing your meal plan with total macros. Eventually it should
> prefill the day's nutrition and prompt accept/deny on whether you actually ate that meal. It
> should also read from saved meals — so uplift the saved-meals section.

---

## Scope split

**Phase 1 (this plan, one implementer session):** create/edit/activate a plan, show it as a section
on Nutrition with per-meal and total macros, the 4-week check-in, and the saved-meals uplift needed
to feed it. Includes **training-day / rest-day variants** (D7) — modelled and shipped together,
because retrofitting them costs a migration and a rework of the setup flow.

**Phase 2 (separate backlog entry, not planned here):** prefill the day's food logs from the active
plan and prompt accept/deny per meal. Deliberately deferred — it writes to `food_logs`, which is an
offline-first synced domain with an outbox path, and getting the write semantics wrong there is the
single most damaging bug class in this project. It deserves its own plan once Phase 1 is in use.

## Decisions, with reasoning

### D1 — Supermarkets: a curated chain picker, NOT geolocation ✅ owner-confirmed 2026-08-11

The request says "checks location and lists local supermarkets". **Recommend against geolocation**
for Phase 1. Instead: a multi-select of AU chains (Coles, Woolworths, Aldi, IGA, Costco, "other"),
defaulted from the user's country, editable at any time.

Reasoning — the store list exists only to bias what the AI suggests ("something you can buy at
Aldi"). Precise coordinates buy nothing over "which chains do you shop at" unless we also hold
per-store product catalogues and stock, which we do not and will not. Against that near-zero
benefit, geolocation costs: a runtime location permission, a new Places-style API key and egress
allowance, and — per **Canonical Runtime** in CLAUDE.md — a location entry in the Play Store
data-safety declaration, on a listing that already faces a declared-use-case review for Health
Connect. Not worth it for a wording nicety.

If store-specific pricing or availability is ever genuinely wanted, revisit with a real product-data
source; geolocation alone would not deliver it.

### D2 — Meal count: recommend 3–4, and be honest that frequency is mostly preference

The owner asked for "a scientifically backed one". The honest position:

- **Meal frequency has little independent effect** on weight change or body composition once daily
  calories and protein are held constant. Any UI that implies otherwise is overclaiming.
- **What does have support is per-meal protein distribution.** Roughly `0.4 g/kg` bodyweight per
  meal, across 3–5 meals, is the common recommendation for maximising the muscle-protein-synthesis
  response across a day.
- So: derive the *suggested* count as `clamp(round(proteinTarget / (0.4 × bodyweightKg)), 3, 5)`,
  present it as a recommendation with a one-line explanation, and let the user pick 1–6 freely.

The copy must say frequency is largely preference and that hitting the daily totals is what matters.
Do not render a number without that caveat — this is the "no LLM self-reported number gates an
action" principle applied to nutrition science.

### D3 — Training-time priming: carbs shift, protein stays even

Given a typical training window, bias **carbohydrate** toward the meals immediately before and after
it, and keep **protein** roughly even across all meals (per D2). Fat is the remainder, and is
skewed *away* from the pre-workout meal. Keep the shift modest — a ±20–30% redistribution of carbs
between meals, never a change to the daily totals. The daily totals are set by the calorie balance
service and are not the AI's to move.

### D4 — Calorie target comes from the energy-balance service, never re-derived

`computeEnergyBalance()` already owns maintenance (calibrated when possible), the goal offset, and
the recommended target. The meal plan reads `target.recommendedKcal` / the saved target and the
current `nutrition_targets` macros. **One Formula, One Place** — a plan that computed its own
target would be a fourth answer to "how much should I eat", which is the exact problem the
2026-08-11 work removed.

"Adjusting for exercise" is already how the balance works: measured movement raises expenditure, so
the day's remaining allowance rises on a training day. The *plan* is built against the daily target;
the *bar* shows the day's live adjustment. Do not bake a second exercise adjustment into the plan.

### D5 — Plans reference saved meals where possible

A plan slot points at either an existing `saved_meals` row or a generated meal. Generated meals are
persisted as `saved_meals` on accept, so the library grows as plans are made and the two features
reinforce rather than duplicate. This is the uplift the owner asked for, and it also gives Phase 2 a
single thing to log.

### D6 — The 4-week check-in is an on-open card, not a scheduled job

**There is no cron layer** (`docs/module-map.md` §0). Copy the existing goals-check-in card pattern
on Home: compute "plan is ≥28 days old" server-side, surface a dismissible card. Never introduce a
scheduler for this.

### D7 — Training-day / rest-day variants: model now, second variant opt-in ✅ owner-decided 2026-08-11

The owner wants training/rest splits and asked whether they should be V2. **Build them now**, but
keep the second variant opt-in.

Reasoning — the cost is asymmetric. Shipping one plan and adding variants later means a migration
to introduce a day type, a rework of the setup flow, and a migration of every existing plan into
the "applies to all days" case. Modelling it upfront costs one nullable column and one extra step
in the flow that only appears if the toggle is on. The *default* stays a single variant, so the
simple case is not made worse.

Shape: `meal_plan_variants` sits between the plan and its meals.

- Every plan has at least one variant, `day_type = 'all'`.
- Toggling "different macros on training days" replaces it with two: `'training'` and `'rest'`.
  Turning it back off collapses to `'all'`, keeping the training variant's meals.
- Which variant applies to a given date comes from the user's **schedule** (`schedules` /
  `schedule_days`) — the same source the rest of the app uses to decide whether today is a training
  day. Do not add a second definition of "training day"; there is already one.
- The rest-day variant is generated at the same target minus the training-day carb allowance, with
  protein held constant (per D2/D3). Daily calorie totals for each variant still come from the
  energy-balance service — the variants differ in composition and total, not in how the target is
  derived.

### D8 — Dietary restrictions: a searchable, structured picker backed by a reference table ✅ owner-decided 2026-08-11

**Revised 2026-08-11** — the owner asked to move away from free text to selectable, searchable
options. That needs real tables, so it is specified here rather than left to implementation.

**Reference table, seeded and global** (`dietary_restrictions`): `code`, `label`, `category`
(`allergen` | `diet_pattern` | `dislike`), `synonyms jsonb`, `sort_order`. Search matches label
*and* synonyms, so "milk" finds Lactose and "shellfish" finds Crustacean. Seeding rules from
CLAUDE.md apply: reference rows by **`code`**, never by name at migration run time, and make the
seed idempotent — `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` only governs fresh databases, so a later correction to a
seeded label needs its own explicit `UPDATE … WHERE` migration.

**User-level, not plan-level** (`user_dietary_restrictions`): `user_id` + `restriction_id` +
`severity` (`avoid` | `allergy`). An allergy is a property of the person, not of one plan, so every
plan and every future AI meal suggestion inherits it automatically. Storing it per-plan would mean
a new plan silently forgets an allergy — the worst possible failure for this feature.

**The plan snapshots the active set at generation time** (`meal_plans.restrictions_snapshot jsonb`)
so an old plan still explains why it looks the way it does after the user edits their restrictions.

**Keep one optional free-text "anything else?" field** — recommended, and the owner may veto. A
fixed taxonomy will always miss something ("no raw fish while pregnant", "onions give me
migraines"), and the alternative to a text box is the user silently getting a plan they cannot eat.
It is explicitly secondary: the structured list is the mechanism, the note is a hint passed to the
model.

**Safety constraint — unchanged and not optional.** Structured input makes the *capture* reliable;
it does not make an LLM's filtering reliable. So:

- The UI must not imply a guarantee — no verified/shield affordance, no "safe for you" copy.
- `severity = 'allergy'` items are rendered back on the review step as an explicit "must not
  contain" list beside each generated meal's ingredients, so accepting *is* the check.
- No automatic action may depend on the model having filtered correctly. This is a further reason
  Phase 2's prefill prompts accept/deny per meal rather than logging silently.
- Consider filtering candidate foods in **code** against a known-ingredient map where one exists,
  rather than trusting the prompt alone. Deterministic where possible, per the repo's rule that
  deterministic maths lives in code.

## Data model

**Migrations `177_meal_plans.sql`** (plans, variants, meals) and **`178_dietary_restrictions.sql`**
(the seeded reference table + the user join table, D8) — claim against the directory *and* open PRs before writing
(known collisions on disk: 081, 087, 146, 161). Also needs a follow-up `179_claude_ro_views_meal_plans.sql`
regenerated via `CLAUDE_RO_OWNER_USER_ID=<id> node scripts/generate-claude-ro-views.js` into a **new**
number — the `claude_ro` schema is default-deny, so new tables are unreadable until they have views,
and a DB-backed test fails if the counts diverge.

```
meal_plans
  id                uuid pk
  user_id           uuid not null → users(id) on delete cascade
  name              text not null
  is_active         boolean not null default false
  meals_per_day     integer not null
  target_calories   integer not null      -- snapshot at generation time
  target_protein_g  double precision not null
  target_carbs_g    double precision not null
  target_fat_g      double precision not null
  training_time     text                  -- 'HH:MM' local, null = no usual time
  stores            jsonb not null default '[]'   -- ['coles','woolworths',…]
  excluded_foods    jsonb not null default '[]'   -- ingredient keys the user rejected
  restrictions_snapshot jsonb not null default '[]'  -- D8: the restriction set at generation time
  avoid_note        text                  -- optional free-text hint, secondary to the picker (D8)
  generated_at      timestamptz not null default now()
  last_reviewed_at  timestamptz           -- drives the 4-week check-in
  created_at        timestamptz not null default now()
  updated_at        timestamptz not null default now()
  deleted_at        timestamptz           -- tombstone (see Sync below)

meal_plan_variants
  id                uuid pk
  meal_plan_id      uuid not null → meal_plans(id) on delete cascade
  day_type          text not null            -- 'all' | 'training' | 'rest'  (D7)
  target_calories   integer not null
  target_protein_g  double precision not null
  target_carbs_g    double precision not null
  target_fat_g      double precision not null
  unique (meal_plan_id, day_type)

meal_plan_meals
  id                uuid pk
  variant_id        uuid not null → meal_plan_variants(id) on delete cascade
  meal_type_id      uuid → meal_types(id) on delete set null
  saved_meal_id     uuid → saved_meals(id) on delete set null
  position          integer not null
  name              text not null
  notes             text
  target_calories   integer not null
  target_protein_g  double precision not null
  target_carbs_g    double precision not null
  target_fat_g      double precision not null
```

**Only one plan may be active per user.** Enforce with a partial unique index, not application
code — `CREATE UNIQUE INDEX meal_plans_one_active ON meal_plans (user_id) WHERE is_active AND deleted_at IS NULL;`
Activating a plan deactivates the rest in the *same transaction*.

Per-meal macros are **stored, not derived at render**, so editing one meal does not silently
reflow the others. A plan-level "totals vs target" delta is computed on read and shown; drift is
surfaced, never auto-corrected.

### Ownership and scoping

- Every query scoped to `user_id`; neither `meal_plan_variants` nor `meal_plan_meals` has a
  `user_id` column, so a write keyed on a client-supplied id must **pre-check ownership by joining
  back to `meal_plans`** — the `ensureWorkoutSession` pattern, now two joins deep. This is the (c)
  case in the write-path ownership rule, and the extra level is exactly where it gets skipped.
- Any PATCH body is Zod-whitelisted before reaching Drizzle `.set()` — never pass a raw body
  (`userId`/`deletedAt` are settable column keys and the TypeScript `Omit<>` is compile-time only).
- After a user-scoped UPDATE whose id came from the client, **check the affected-row count** before
  any dependent child write. A 0-row match followed by an unscoped child delete is a cross-user wipe.

## Sync / offline

Phase 1 is **read-mostly on device**: plans are created and edited in a flow that needs the AI route
anyway, so it is online-only, and the *display* is what must work offline.

- Add a local table mirroring `meal_plans` + `meal_plan_meals`, hydrated from the pull delta, so
  the Nutrition section renders the active plan offline. Register both in `RECONCILE_TABLES` /
  `RECONCILE_COLUMNS` **in the same commit** as the local migration (local SQLite is at v22 — the
  next version is v23, and `lib/sqlite/__tests__/migrations.test.ts` asserts the max).
- The local table must hold everything needed to **render** — meal names and macros, not just ids.
  A plan that stores only `saved_meal_id` repeats the `food_logs` → `food_items` data-loss bug.
- `deleted_at` tombstones on `meal_plans`, emitted by `getSyncDelta`, or a delete on one device
  never propagates.
- Add the `mealPlans` domain flag to `pullDelta` and the sync-provider group mapping in the same PR.
- If a write path is added later (Phase 2, or offline editing), it needs an outbox domain and a
  `pushMutations` branch calling **the same shared function** as the web route.

## API

| Route | Notes |
|---|---|
| `GET /api/nutrition/meal-plans` | list (excludes tombstoned) |
| `POST /api/nutrition/meal-plans` | create from a generated draft |
| `GET /api/nutrition/meal-plans/[id]` | one plan + meals |
| `PATCH /api/nutrition/meal-plans/[id]` | rename / activate / edit meals; Zod-whitelisted |
| `DELETE /api/nutrition/meal-plans/[id]` | tombstone, not hard delete |
| `POST /api/nutrition/meal-plans/generate` | **AI** — returns a draft, persists nothing |
| `POST /api/nutrition/meal-plans/[id]/review` | records a check-in outcome, stamps `last_reviewed_at` |

- `generate` uses `generateObject` with a response schema — never `JSON.parse` of model text.
- It gets **the standard rate limit at creation**, matching sibling AI routes.
- Deterministic maths stays in code: the AI proposes foods and portions; the calorie/macro split
  across meals is computed by a shared helper in `packages/shared/src/nutrition/meal-split.ts`, and
  the returned plan is **validated and clamped** against the daily target before it is shown
  (same shape as `clampRecommendation`). No LLM-reported total is trusted.
- GET routes ship `Cache-Control: private, no-store` — **Q-166 resolved against SWR headers while
  this plan sat in the queue**, and `scripts/check-api-no-store.js` now fails CI on them. The app's
  own cache handles freshness. Client reads go
  through `cachedFetch` with a `readCacheSync` seed. Register the keys in a
  `invalidateMealPlans()` group in `lib/cache-groups.ts` — never a hand-rolled key list at the
  write site.

## Shared logic (`packages/shared/src/nutrition/`)

- `meal-split.ts` — `suggestMealCount(proteinG, bodyweightKg)` (D2) and
  `splitMacrosAcrossMeals(targets, mealCount, trainingTime, mealTimes)` (D3). Pure, unit-tested,
  including: totals always reconcile to the daily target within rounding, protein stays even, the
  carb shift never changes daily totals, and a null training time produces an even split.

## UI

- **Nutrition tab**: an "Active plan" section under the energy-balance bar showing each meal with
  its macros, plan totals, and a totals-vs-target delta. Collapsible; hidden entirely when no plan
  is active.
- **Setup flow**: a multi-step sheet (stores → dietary restrictions → excluded foods → meal count →
  training time → generate → review/edit → save). Reuse `components/ui/` primitives; grep before
  adding a new one.
- **Restrictions picker**: a searchable multi-select over `dietary_restrictions`, matching label and
  synonyms, grouped by category, with an allergy/avoid toggle per selection. It is reachable from
  Profile too — it is user-level data (D8), not something you should have to build a plan to edit.
- **Saved-meals uplift** (`components/nutrition/saved-meals-sheet.tsx`): search/filter, per-meal
  macro totals, edit and delete, and "add to plan". Note the file is one of the **9 remaining
  hand-rolled chevron toggles missing `aria-expanded`** — fix it while in there.
- Safe-area: the setup sheet is a bottom sheet, so `SheetContent side="bottom"`/`SheetFooter` own
  the bottom inset — **never** add `pb-safe*` inside it. Any full-screen takeover step uses
  `pb-safe-action-lg`.
- Theme tokens, not hex literals; Lucide icons, not emoji; colour always paired with a label.
- Cache-seed the section synchronously (`readCacheSync`) — a skeleton on a repeat visit is a bug.

## AI coach integration

- New tool `getMealPlan` in `lib/ai-chat/tools.ts`, returning the active plan and its
  totals-vs-target delta, so the coach can answer "what should I eat tonight" against the real plan.
- The 4-week review should call the same `computeEnergyBalance` service so its verdict cannot
  contradict the widget — if calibrated maintenance has moved, the review proposes a regenerated
  plan at the new target.
- Consider a `mealPlan` writable coach domain later (`lib/coach/domains/`), so the coach can
  propose a plan swap through the existing preview/apply/consequence machinery rather than a
  bespoke path. Not Phase 1.

## Testing

- Unit: `meal-split.ts` (totals reconcile, protein evenness, carb shift bounded, no-training-time
  case, 1-meal and 6-meal edges).
- Unit: collapsing two variants back to `'all'` keeps the training variant's meals and drops the
  rest variant, rather than silently emptying the plan.
- DB-backed: the `(meal_plan_id, day_type)` unique constraint rejects a duplicate variant; deleting
  a plan cascades through variants to meals.
- DB-backed (`lib/data/postgres/__tests__/`): the one-active-plan index actually rejects a second
  active plan; activation is transactional; `meal_plan_meals` writes reject a plan id owned by
  another user; delete tombstones rather than removing rows.
- Route: Zod rejects an unknown/privileged field in PATCH; the generate route clamps an
  out-of-range AI response.
- DB-backed: the `dietary_restrictions` seed is idempotent across a re-run and resolves by `code`;
  a user's restrictions survive a plan delete (they are user-level, not plan-level).
- Local store: schema v23 migration is idempotent under partial application, and the new tables are
  in `RECONCILE_TABLES`/`RECONCILE_COLUMNS`.
- Device: the setup sheet's insets and the Nutrition section are **not** verifiable in the sandbox —
  they need the S25 smoke run, or a Known-Issues row marking them unverified.

## Open questions

**Resolved with the owner 2026-08-11:** supermarkets are a chain picker, not geolocation (D1);
training/rest variants ship in Phase 1 with the second variant opt-in (D7); dietary restrictions are
free text plus suggestion chips, never presented as a guaranteed filter (D8).

**Still open, decide during implementation:**

1. **Should a generated meal be saved to the meal library automatically, or only on request?**
   Taking automatic (D5) unless it proves noisy — it makes the library useful without extra work.
   If it does get noisy, the fix is a "generated" filter in the saved-meals sheet, not a prompt.
2. **What happens to the active plan when calibrated maintenance moves mid-cycle?** The 4-week
   review is the intended moment to regenerate, but a large mid-cycle shift (say >200 kcal) arguably
   should not wait three weeks. Suggest: leave the plan alone, and let the energy-balance bar carry
   the live truth — the plan is a template, not a daily instruction. Revisit if it feels stale in use.
