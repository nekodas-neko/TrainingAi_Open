# Case study — the night of 2026-07-25 → 26, and why nothing caught it

**Owner report:** *"That was the night I got very bad sleep; and felt really bad/tired all of the 26th."*

This walks the whole chain for that one night — raw ring signals → sleep session → daily summary →
Sleep Score / Readiness / Illness / Body Battery — against what the owner actually logged, to find
which model should have caught it and did not. Companion to
[`2026-07-27-prod-data-audit-2-derived-metrics.md`](2026-07-27-prod-data-audit-2-derived-metrics.md);
findings that generalise beyond this night are queued there (Q-1) and here (Q-15).

All scores below were produced by **running the real modules** (`computeSleepScore`,
`illnessFromSummaries`, `computeReadinessComposite`) against the production rows — no formula was
reimplemented.

---

## 1. It was a genuinely bad night — just not in any dimension the Sleep Score measures

Main sleep **2026-07-25 21:54 → 2026-07-26 05:19** (Brisbane), 7.00 h.

| Signal | This night | Previous 3 nights | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `average_hrv_ms` | **34.0** | 67.5 / 53.0 / 50.0 | **−2.76 σ** vs the 49.5 ms baseline — the **lowest HRV of the entire BLE era** |
| `avg_heart_rate` | **72** | 62 / 62 / 62 | **+10 bpm**, a 16% rise |
| Wake time | **05:19** | 07:02 / 07:01 / 07:22 | **~2 h early** |
| `rem_sleep_hours` | **1.17** | 1.33 / 2.00 / 2.08 | ~45% below the good nights |
| `duration_hours` | 7.00 | 7.42 / 9.00 / 8.17 | normal-ish |
| `efficiency` | **94** | 94 / 96 / 92 | **normal** |
| `deep_sleep_hours` | 1.00 | 0.83 / 1.08 / 1.33 | **normal** |
| `onset_latency_sec` | 600 | 600 / 900 / 600 | **normal** |
| `restless_periods` | 3 | 3 / 1 / 2 | **normal** |

The hourly HR curve says the same thing — it never settled:

```

22:00  n=12  min=74  avg=78.3
```

A normal night for this user troughs mid-night around 52–54 bpm and holds. This one entered sleep at
~86 bpm, bottomed out at 63.7 an hour before an early wake, and never reached the usual floor.

**Everything the Sleep Score looks at was normal. Everything that was abnormal was autonomic.**

## 2. The owner told the app, in the app, and it changed nothing

| Log | 2026-07-23 | 07-24 | 07-25 | **07-26** | 07-27 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `day_checkins.sleep_quality_feel` (1 = slept great … **5 = terrible**) | 2 | 2 | 2 | **5** | 2 |
| `mood_logs.energy_level` | ok | good | ok | **drained** | good |

Both are the only outlier in the window. **`sleep_quality_feel = 5` feeds nothing quantitative** —
its only consumer is the AI prescription *prompt* as free text
(`lib/ai-periodization/prompt.ts:254`). No score reads it. `energy_level` does feed readiness via
`checkinScoreFromEnergy`, so "drained" was counted there — but nothing carried it into the Sleep
Score.

No behavioural cause is recoverable from the data: no food logged on 07-25 at all, and
`late_heavy_meal` / `hydration` / `barely_moved` / `physical_tiredness` / `mental_drain` are **NULL on
every check-in in the window**. The evening check-in — where those live — has only **4 rows in the
entire history** against 24 morning rows. The 07-25 workout was unremarkable (Lower, 08:40–09:36,
RPE 7, 4,470 kg).

## 3. What each surface actually produced

| Surface | Shipped value | Should it have flagged the night? |
|---|---|---|
| **Sleep Score** | **80** | ❌ No. Re-running the real model on the real night gives **82** — correct arithmetic, and *not* nap-contaminated. The model simply has no autonomic input. |
| **Readiness** | **29** | ⚠️ Right number, wrong reason — see §4. |
| **Illness radar** | **watch**, score 43 | ⚠️ Same — artefact-driven. |
| **Body Battery** | flat **29** all day, charged 0, drained 0 | ❌ No. It did literally nothing — see §5. |

### The Sleep Score, contributor by contributor

> **Correction (2026-07-27).** An earlier revision of this document said the `hrv` contributor "was
> not supplied". That was wrong for the route that produces the displayed score:
> `app/api/readiness-score/route.ts` **does** pass `hrvBaselineMs`. Reproducing its exact 28-day
> window confirms the contributor fired and scored 57/100. The real defects are different and
> narrower — they are (1) and (2) below. The corrected numbers are used throughout.

```
REAL night, no HRV contributor (what 4 of the 6 callers do):  82
  totalSleep=76  efficiency=96  rem=75  deep=78  latency=98  timing=82  restfulness=88

REAL night, hrv supplied with the route's own baseline (42.95 ms):  79   [persisted: 80]
  … hrv=57 …
```

Reproducing the route's baseline over its real 28-day window:

| HRV baseline used | mean | `hrv` sub-score | night's score |
|---|---|---|---|
| none — `body-battery`, `weekly-digest`, `sleep-trend`, the resilience rollup | — | absent | **82** |
| what the route actually used (naps included, both eras) | 42.95 ms | 57 | **79** (persisted 80) |
| naps excluded | 42.12 ms | 59 | 79 |
| naps excluded **and BLE-era only** | **48.34 ms** | **44** | **78** |

Three things fall out:

1. **Six callers, two answers.** Only `readiness-score` and the score audit passed a baseline; the
   other four (`body-battery`'s fallback anchor, `weekly-digest`, `sleep-trend`, the resilience
   rollup) passed none, so the *same night* scored 82 on those surfaces and 80 on the Health screen.
   That is a One-Formula-One-Place violation, not a missing feature.
2. **The baseline itself was contaminated.** The 28-day window on 2026-07-26 still reached back to
   2026-06-28, so it held 10 Cloud-era nights whose HRV is on a different scale (**Q-4**) — dragging
   the baseline down 6 ms, which *flatters* the ratio and blunts the very signal it exists to catch.
   Naps mattered far less than expected (0.8 ms).
3. **Even fully corrected, 78 is not a "bad night" number**, because `hrv` carried weight 12 of 100 —
   and **there was no heart-rate contributor at all**, so +10 bpm of overnight HR was invisible. The
   two signals that actually distinguished this night were worth at most 12% of the score between
   them. That, not the plumbing, is the real gap.

## 4. Readiness 29 was right for the wrong reason — and fixing Q-1 alone will make it *worse*

Per **Q-1**, `oura_daily_summary` for 2026-07-26 holds the **45-minute, 0.00 h artefact session**
(`hrv 25.0`, `rhr_low 73.4`, `breath 6.7`), not the real night. Running the composite both ways:

| Input | rhrZ | hrvZ | Readiness | Illness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The artefact** (what shipped) | **+2.57** | **−4.48** | **27** (≈ the 29 persisted) | **watch**, 43 |
| **The real night** | +0.10 | **−2.76** | **46** | **normal**, 36 |

So the app landed near the owner's actual experience by reading a garbage row. **Fix Q-1 in isolation
and readiness for 2026-07-26 moves 29 → 46, and the illness flag drops from `watch` to `normal`** —
the app becomes *less* aligned with how the owner felt, not more.

That is the single most important finding here: **Q-1 and the Sleep-Score calibration below must land
together**, or the Q-1 fix will read as a regression on exactly the day that prompted this review.

Note also that the real night's `rhrZ` is +0.10 — resting HR was genuinely fine. The night's tell was
HRV (−2.76 σ) and *average* HR, not resting HR. The composite's `restingHeartRate` contributor uses
`rhr_low_bpm`, so it saw nothing.

## 5. Body Battery did nothing on the day the owner felt drained

```
date        anchor  src        end  min  max  charged  drained  hr_sample_count
2026-07-25  60      readiness  62   60   83   24       22       1497
2026-07-26  29      readiness  29   29   29    0        0          0     ← flat all day
2026-07-27  87      readiness  93   72  100   37       28       1582
```

It sat at the readiness anchor (29) from midnight to midnight with **zero charge and zero drain**.
The row was `computed_at` 05:16 and `updated_at` 19:39 Brisbane, so it *did* re-run during the day and
still consumed nothing.

**This is not a missing-data problem.** `oura_heartrate` holds **235 readings across 23 distinct hours**
for 2026-07-26. The one thing that distinguishes that date: it is the **only day in the window whose HR
is ring-only** (`source = ble`; 07-25 and 07-27 both have `ble` + `chest_strap`). So Body Battery
consumed 0 of 235 available ring readings on a day it had near-full-day coverage.

Because the anchor is readiness, and readiness came from the artefact (§4), Body Battery's 29 also
inherits Q-1 — and with 0 charge/drain it is *purely* that number, re-displayed.

## 6. The artefact also poisoned `body_metrics`

A propagation path not recorded in Q-1's original write-up:

```
body_metrics 2026-07-26:  resting_heart_rate = 73,  hrv_ms = 25.0     ← the artefact's values
sleep_sessions (real night):  lowest_heart_rate = 60,  average_hrv_ms = 34.0
```

`body_metrics.resting_heart_rate` is the input to `resolveHrProfile`'s fixed 28-day RHR mean, which
sets **every HR-zone boundary** for `/api/zone-minutes` and the cardio hub — and it is what the HRV
and resting-HR trend charts render. So one nap-shaped artefact moved the zone boundaries and put a
false spike in two trend charts.

## What to change (owner's call — these are alternatives, not a plan)

### A. Give the Sleep Score an autonomic axis — the actual gap ✅ SHIPPED 2026-07-27

Owner-approved and implemented in the same session this document was written; see §8 for the
before/after over the whole history.

### B. Use the subjective rating you already collect — owner decision: **historical only**

`sleep_quality_feel = 5` ("Terrible") was logged on the morning of the 26th and no score reads it.
The owner's call (2026-07-27) is that it stays **out** of the score and becomes a historical
calibration record instead — something to look back on when tuning, not an input. Queued as Q-16.

### C. Fix Body Battery's ring-only blind spot

Find why 235 ring readings across 23 hours yielded `hr_sample_count = 0` while chest-strap days yield
1,500+. Until then, a day without the strap produces a Body Battery that is just yesterday's readiness
number held flat — which is worse than showing nothing, because it looks like a measurement.

### D. Sequencing — this matters ✅ RESOLVED

**Q-1 must not land before A.** Fixing the nap/artefact selection moves this day's readiness 29 → 46
and clears the illness flag. If the Sleep Score has not gained an autonomic contributor by then, the
app will score the owner's worst night of the month as *better* than it does today. **A shipped
first (2026-07-27), so Q-1 is now safe to implement.**

### E. Make the evening check-in reachable

4 evening rows against 24 morning rows, and `late_heavy_meal` / `hydration` / `mental_drain` NULL
throughout, means the fields that would explain a night like this are structurally empty. No amount of
model work recovers a cause that was never captured.

## 8. What shipped (2026-07-27) and what it does to the whole history

Owner-approved the same day. Four changes to `lib/health/sleep-score.ts` and its six callers:

1. **One shared baseline derivation** — `sleepScoreBaselines(priorSessions, tz)`. Every caller now
   goes through it, so the Health screen, weekly digest, sleep trend, body-battery anchor and score
   audit can no longer disagree about the same night. It filters to main sleeps
   (`MAIN_SLEEP_MIN_HOURS = 4`) so naps and rest fragments — whose HRV/HR are taken awake — stay out
   of a *sleep* baseline. `computeSleepScoreSeries()` scores a whole history, each night against the
   nights that precede it.
2. **`hr` contributor (weight 14)** — overnight average HR vs personal baseline, the mirror of `hrv`.
   100 at or below your own norm, falling away above it.
3. **`schedule` contributor (weight 8)** — bed/wake vs habitual, **directional**: only a *late*
   bedtime or an *early* wake is penalised, worst endpoint wins. An early night or a lie-in is
   neutral (duration and circadian timing already price those). The symmetric version marked
   2026-07-27 down 94 → 89 for going to bed early after a bad night, which is the opposite of the
   intended signal — see the curve comment for the reasoning.
4. **Rebalanced weights** — sleep-architecture terms trimmed to fund the two new ones. Autonomic
   state (`hrv` + `hr`) is now **28 of 110 (25%)** rather than 12 of 100 (12%). `totalSleep` remains
   the largest single term at 24.

regression test, since that was the owner's explicit constraint.

### Verification

local Postgres seeded with 14 nights plus a reconstruction of the 2026-07-25 night: `/api/readiness-score`
returned all ten contributors and scored it **71** — matching the production sweep exactly —
`/api/body-battery`'s anchor agreed at **71** (it would previously have said 82), `/api/weekly-digest`
returned 200, and `/api/admin/day-review` rendered all ten contributors with effective weights
summing to 1.0.

**Not exercised:** anything on-device. Native SQLite, the APK's WebView rendering of the two new
