# 2026-08-24 — LA-21 measured: eleven sessions, eight to fourteen hours each

**Branch:** `docs/la-21-measured` · **Lane A** · docs only.

## Correcting my own filing

I filed LA-21 a few hours earlier, after a 1,176-minute session on the **dev** database produced a
training load 24× a normal one. The entry said: *"Not a live corruption; it needs a session that was
started and not ended, and the owner's production rows do not currently show one."*

**They show eleven.** Of 81 completed sessions, **11 (13.6%) span 534–845 minutes — 8.9 to 14.1
hours.** I filed severity from a dev fixture instead of asking production, which took one query.

p50 is **56 minutes**, p90 is **548**. **Nothing sits between 92 and 534 minutes.** That empty gap is
the finding: these are not the tail of a distribution, they are a different phenomenon.

## They are real workouts, and that decides the fix

Each of the eleven carries **5–6 exercises, 13–18 sets, 3,700–7,400 kg of volume**. They are complete
training sessions whose *duration* is wrong — not abandoned starts.

So **clamp, don't exclude.** My original entry guessed the opposite for the load series ("excluding is
probably right"), reasoning about a hypothetical abandoned session. Excluding would delete training
that happened.

## They stopped, and that is not the same as being fixed

Every one falls between **2026-05-04 and 2026-05-29**. None since. CLAUDE.md is explicit that
something which stopped on its own is recorded as *unexplained*, not closed — so the cap should bound
the number regardless of cause, and the cause stays open.

The client already handles the case you would guess: `workout-store.ts:215` drops a session's entire
identity on rehydrate once its start anchor is over four hours old or from a previous day, so an
app-kill cannot produce one. What is left is the app being left **open** across a long gap with
Complete tapped at the end — `resolveCompletedAt` takes the phone's own `completedAtMs` and only
rejects it for preceding the start or being in the future. Plausible; not proven; does not explain the
one-month cluster.

## Live reach is narrower than 13.6% sounds

Worth knowing before pricing the fix, and each part was checked rather than assumed:

- **None of the eleven carries a session RPE or a single rated set**, so they are invisible to
  `health-trends`' `sessionLoad` series. I checked this specifically against Q-420's derivation, which
  merged hours earlier and reads set RPEs — it does **not** pull them in. That was the regression I
  was looking for in my own change, and it is not there.
- **`app/api/body-metadata` reads only *today's* workouts**, so the day-energy path cannot reach a May
  session.
- What remains is the per-session views — `workout-sessions/[id]/energy` and the recap — where opening
  one of the eleven shows a calorie estimate built on a 10× duration.

High prevalence, narrow live reach, unknown cause. The prevalence argues for bounding it; the reach
argues for not rushing.
