Six data domains, one day, one screen — the layout is the whole problem.

The backlog item already exists and is well specified. What it deliberately leaves open is the thing you actually asked for: what this screen looks like. It says, in its own words, "don't default to a flat scroll of stat tiles (the current sheet's weakness) — with this much data per day, the layout itself is a real design problem." So these mockups are about structure, not decoration: exercise, HR, sleep, twelve body-composition fields, four scores and logged activities all belong to one day, and the decision is what leads, what groups, and what stays folded.

It becomes a screen, not a sheet

Q-110's second round supersedes the first: a real route you swipe left/right between days on, "kinda like the food logging" — not more sections crammed into the bottom sheet.

The swipe is already written

nutrition-content.tsx has the exact pattern — useDrag, shiftDateStr, AnimatePresence keyed on the date. The plan mandates copying it rather than hand-rolling a third swipe.

Five fields are already wasted

DayBodyMeta fetches bodyFat, protein, carb, fat and distanceKm today and renders none of them. That part is free.

Scores have a trap

They must come through buildDayAudit, never a direct oura_daily read — those Cloud columns are frozen since the 2026-07-07 BLE re-key and would silently show wrong history.

Where I'd go

A1 Chronological, with B4's week strip on top. It is the only layout that answers "what did I do on Friday" instead of "what data exists for Friday", and a day genuinely is a sequence — you slept, then trained, then walked, then weighed in. It also degrades best: a day with only a weigh-in is one row, not five empty section headers. The week strip earns its place separately by making the swipe discoverable — a bare date gives no hint the screen moves sideways.

A3 Pinned summary is the one to take seriously if you disagree. Its argument is real: in every scrolling layout, once you are down in the exercise list you have lost all sense of what kind of day this was.

A5 Tabbed I would not build, despite the density being tempting. Horizontal swipe changes the day, and tabs invite horizontal swipe — two gestures competing on one axis. This app has already lost a session to exactly that class of bug twice with pull-to-sync.

Two things worth deciding before implementation, not during

1. Does the full HR chart come with? The plan recommends starting with summary stats (resting HR / HRV / SpO₂) and adding a full-day chart later. But the per-session HR chart already exists and is the most interesting thing on the current sheet — A1 and A2 both keep it. A separate question is the full 24-hour trace from /api/oura/hr-day, which no mockup here includes; it is a third chart on an already-dense screen.

2. What happens on an empty day? Every mockup shows a rich Friday. Most days will have a weigh-in and steps and nothing else. A1 handles that best by construction; the sectioned layouts need an explicit empty state per section or the screen becomes five headers over nothing.
