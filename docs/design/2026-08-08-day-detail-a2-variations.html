Health · day detail — A2 with real depth

Scores on top, the day underneath — six ways to fill it in.

A2's structure kept: the four scores lead, sections follow. What changes is what's in them — the full five-exercise list with set, rep and weight columns, session totals, the HR trace, and the twelve-field body-composition card at the bottom. Each mockup is rendered at its full content height with the 891 dp fold marked, because with this much detail the real question is what you see before scrolling.

Swipe is in all six

Every variation moves between days. C4 shows the treatment I'd pick — a week strip instead of chevrons, which makes the gesture discoverable and shows which nearby days have data.

The fold is the decision

C1 puts the exercise list above it and everything else below. C2 fits the whole day above it. C6 splits the difference. Same content, three different answers to "what do I see when I land".

Nothing here is decoration

Every number shown maps to a real column — the exercise rows to set_logs, the twelve body fields to body_metrics, the HR trace to the chart that already exists in the sheet.

Where I'd go

C2 collapsible, with C4's week strip. It is the only one where the whole day is visible before you scroll — which is what makes swiping between days actually useful, since you land and read the shape of the day in one glance rather than scrolling each one. Training stays open because it is the headline; Sleep and Body composition are one line until you want them.

If you'd rather never tap to reveal, C6 compact is the honest alternative: everything always visible, at the cost of the day reading like a table.

C5 flat is worth a look regardless of which you pick — it removes a real redundancy. In C1 every section is a bordered card sitting inside a padded screen, which is two borders doing one job, and it is the treatment furthest from the Band/Divider language you chose for Home.

Two things these mockups assume that the API does not do yet

Session totals and per-set weights. The volume/avg-HR/peak-HR row and the set × rep × weight columns are shown as if available per exercise. DayExercise already carries setWeights, sets and reps, so the columns are real — but volume is not returned and would need deriving, and avg/peak HR would come from the same source the existing HR chart uses.

The weekly weight delta. "−0.4 kg this week" needs a second query the day route doesn't make. Cheap, but it is not free, and it is the kind of thing that quietly turns a one-fetch screen into a three-fetch one — which matters here, because this screen must paint instantly from cache when you swipe between days.
