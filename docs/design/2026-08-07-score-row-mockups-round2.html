Twenty more, built from what you kept — and what you threw out.

Seven picks out of twenty-eight, and the pattern in them is sharper than the picks themselves: every hard-stroke instrument went in the bin. All ten filled dials, the gauge, the bezel, the segmented ring, the progress arc — gone. What survived was soft light, tinted surface, and density. So none of the twenty below draws a ring, a stroke, or an arc. They pull harder on the three things that did survive.

Light, not line

Halo and Watermark both work by glow and field rather than by edge. Family E takes that further: light that pools, casts, and drifts, with nothing ever outlined.

The icon is the graphic

Watermark and Bare both said the glyph doesn't need a container to hold the cell together. Family F makes the icon the composition instead of a marker sitting above a number.

Surface over frame

Squircle and Frosted are both materials — tint and glass. Family G explores what else that material can do: rim light, recess, gradient, blob.

Height is the real currency

Picking both Pill and Rail says the row is too tall. Family H goes further down: 44dp, and a version where all four share a single line.

What round two deliberately does not do

No design here encodes the value. Round one offered ten that did; you kept none of them, so I've stopped proposing them rather than re-serving the same idea in new clothes. The consequence is worth stating plainly once: in every design below, a Readiness of 91 and a Readiness of 34 look identical apart from the digits. That's a legitimate choice — the number is the information and the row is a launchpad, not a dashboard — but it means the row can never be glanced at, only read.

If you ever want the middle ground, G5 Split and H5 Two-tier are the two here with somewhere obvious to put a band word later without a redesign.
