Upper · 5 exercises

A — one strip, recommended. The four notifications become a single row showing what is waiting and how many. Tapping expands them inline; it does not navigate away. It is the only option that reliably puts a real card in the first screen, and the four are all the same kind of thing — “ready for you” — so grouping them is honest rather than a trick to save height.

B — thin rows. Each keeps its own line, tappable and dismissable in one touch, at roughly half the height of today's banner. Better if you act on these from Home rather than reading them; worse on a busy morning, when four lines still push the first card down.

The honest cost of A: an item behind a tap is an item you may not act on. If the day-in-review prompt is something you use daily, A makes it slower. B costs height; A costs a tap — and which is worse depends on whether you treat these as a to-do list or as noise.

Heights are drawn to scale relative to one another, not measured on the device. The stack is recreated from session-select-content.tsx:1128–1192 — a live screenshot needs the app with all six conditions true at once, which no sandbox can arrange.

What I need: A, B, or a change. This is the only open question — the two-stay / four-collapse split is already yours from 2026-09-22 and is unchanged here.

Reversal cost is low either way: one component, no stored data. Whichever you pick gets committed here so it cannot be lost a second time.
