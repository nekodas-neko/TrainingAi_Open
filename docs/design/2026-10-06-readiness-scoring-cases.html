How readiness reaches 100, and which baselines it uses

Companion to the Readiness hub mockup (#2356). Part 1 runs the shipped readiness function (computeReadinessComposite, model v5, 2026-10-06) on test days. Part 2 is the proposed pillar model. Part 3 covers baselines today and the proposed 1d → 7d → 30d → 90d windows.

1 · Today's model: it cannot reach 100

Each signal is scored against your personal baseline: 50 means "at your normal". It climbs linearly to 80 at +0.9σ, then flattens so extreme days keep their order (TN-60). These rows are the real function's output:

Two things stop it reaching 100.

① Temperature tops out at 90. A perfectly normal temperature should be its best score. The tail compression meant for extreme days also squashes it, so it costs 1.1 readiness points every day. That's a defect, not a choice: a fix candidate in its own right.

② The tails approach 100 but never reach it. By design, an integer 100 needs roughly 23σ. So 98 is the practical ceiling, and even a +3σ day shows 97.

A "typical" day reads 67, not 50, because last night, activity and recovery are already-good absolute scores rather than "vs normal".

2 · Proposed pillar model: 100 is rare, but reachable

Readiness = Σ (pillar × weight). Each pillar = Σ (component × weight). Every component uses one curve, scored against your 30-day normal. The numbers are a proposal for your sign-off:

Where you are vs your 30-day normalComponent scoreReads as
−1.5σ or worse0–25well below your normal
−0.5σ60a little off
at your normal (0σ)70a normal day reads 70, not 50
+1σ90clearly better than usual
+1.5σ or better100as good as you get, and it does reach 100

The curve is 70 + 20·z through the middle, reaching an integer 100 at +1.5σ. Below 25 it flattens, so the worst days keep their order (the reason TN-60 exists). Temperature is scored "closer is better", so 100 at your normal. Fuel and other targets use their research ranges (docs/architecture/component-references.md).

How to hit 100: every component at least 1.5σ better than your 30-day normal on the same morning. Expect a handful of days a year. A 100 means something, but it isn't impossible.

Body reads 78 on a typical day because temperature is 100 at your normal and fuel scores against fixed targets. Test-case numbers are illustrative until the weights proposal is run on your real history.

3 · Baselines: what "your normal" means

Today

There is effectively one baseline per signal: an exponential moving average ported from the ring firmware (personal-baseline.ts). It moves fast for the first 14 nights, then settles to a 1/32 step, a memory of about a month, with each night weighing less the older it is. Nothing scores until 14 nights exist (BASELINE_MIN_NIGHTS). A few signals also use fixed windows: a 28-day average for the HRV and resting-HR displays, and 7-vs-28-day for training load. None of them show you a trend across windows.

Proposed: four windows per signal, one of them scores

1 day
vs yesterday

The arrow on each row: did it move overnight? Context only, never scored. One night is noise.

7 days
this week

Flags a run: HRV 1σ under your 30-day normal for 7 days raises "you're run down" before one bad night would.

30 days · scores
your normal

The reference every component is scored against. Long enough to be stable, short enough to follow real fitness change.

90 days
your season

Drift: is your normal itself rising or falling? It catches slow detraining, the way the 28:90 load ratio did (#2340).

Each component row shows them as one line, for example HRV 38 ms · vs 1d ↑2 · 7d ↓4 · 30d ↓8 · 90d ↓3. A 365-day window joins once a year of history exists (season-over-season).

Rules that keep it honest

· Only 30d scores. The others inform the arrows, alerts and insights, so the number you see has one meaning.
· Learning period: until a signal has 14 nights it shows "learning · day N of 14", never a fake 50. Until 30 nights exist, scoring uses what there is, flagged as provisional.
· Low-wear nights and nap-fragments are excluded from every window, so one missing night can't drag your normal (the nap bug, #2192).
· Per-person refits (the #2341 pattern) tune the curve's width per component and are shadow-tested before they go live.

What needs your yes

① The curve: 70 at your normal, 100 at +1.5σ. ② 30 days as the scoring window, with 1d/7d/90d as context. ③ Then the pillar and component weights, as a tuning proposal with the number of past days that move. The temperature-caps-at-90 defect gets fixed regardless.
