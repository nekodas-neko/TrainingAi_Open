Home screen · score row · round three

Minimal, meaning less — not softer.

Round two answered "what you liked" with atmosphere: glow, glass, blobs, rim light. That was the wrong read. Adding light is still adding. Everything below works the other way — the only moves are type, alignment, hierarchy, spacing, and deletion. No glow, no fills, no arcs, no bars. Four of them have no decoration at all and are held together purely by where things sit.

No fill of any kind

You're right that a bar makes no sense for heart rate — 52 bpm isn't 52% of anything, and faking a denominator for it would have been the one dishonest thing in the set. Nothing here fills, fractions, or ranks.

The icon is the only colour

In most of these it is also the only non-text element on screen. That's the constraint doing real work: strip everything else and the glyph stops being a badge and becomes punctuation.

Whitespace counts as a design

L5 and M4 change nothing but the gaps and the rules. They're in here because at this level of restraint, spacing is the decision.

If you want one recommendation

K2 Baseline is the one I'd build. It is the only design in three rounds where the four cells stop being four separate objects — a single horizontal line runs across the row and the icons hang off it as marks. It deletes the most (no frame, no fill, no container, no decoration), it survives the Samsung compositor with nothing to composite, and it's the shortest non-compact option at ~86dp.

The honest counter-argument: it puts four unrelated numbers on one visual line, which implies they're comparable. They aren't — 52 bpm and 90 sleep have nothing to do with each other. M4 Divider is the same restraint with that problem fixed, at the cost of three hairlines.
