Gym App Navigation Patterns

Three bottom-nav approaches used by leading fitness apps. Each optimises for a different primary user intent — choose based on what action you want users to reach fastest.

✓ Immediately learnable

All 5 sections visible at once. No hunting for where things are. Users grasp the whole app on first glance.

⚠ Crowded on large phones

5 equal tabs gives each only ~52px — tight for tap targets. Far-left tab requires a thumb stretch on S25 Ultra.

⚠ No visual hierarchy

"Start Workout" and "View Nutrition" appear equally important. Users don't know what the app wants them to do.

✓ Action-first hierarchy

The glowing FAB is the first thing the eye sees. "Start workout" is the #1 action — it should be the #1 visual. Used by Hevy and Strong for exactly this reason.

✓ 4 tabs is the sweet spot

Plenty of width per tab (~62px). Easy thumb reach. Cognitive load research shows 4 is the limit before users start forgetting what's where.

✓ Future-proof for Nutrition

Nutrition tab is present but can show a "coming soon" placeholder — sets the expectation without blocking the launch.

✓ Premium, distinctive feel

The floating pill is visually unique. Content scrolls behind it and the transparent blur creates depth. Feels expensive — like WHOOP or Oura.

✓ More usable screen space

No hard edge — content visible to the very bottom of the screen. Works especially well for the activity-ring hero layout.

⚠ No primary action emphasis

Without a FAB, "Start Workout" is buried in the scroll area. Works better for data-viewing apps (WHOOP) than action-first apps (logging reps).

Verdict for TrainingAI

Pattern B is the best fit ⚡

Your app is action-first — users open it to log reps, not passively review data. The central FAB puts "Start Workout" permanently in the thumb zone, where Hevy and Strong have placed it for exactly this reason. 4 tabs avoids the crowding of Pattern A on the S25 Ultra's wide display, and the standard nav bar (vs. floating pill) is better suited to an app where fast, eyes-down tapping matters more than visual prestige.
